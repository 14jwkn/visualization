## Assignment 2: Good and Bad Data Visualization

### Requirements:

- Data visualizations are important tools for communication and convincing; we need to be able to evaluate the ways that data are presented in visual form to be critical consumers of information 
- To test your evaluation skills, locate two public data visualizations online, one good and one bad  
    - You can find data visualizations at https://public.tableau.com/app/discover or https://datavizproject.com/, or anywhere else you like! 
- For each visualization (good and bad):  
    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
    ```
    
    Reference: Material covered up to date.

    Good Visualization:
    https://public.tableau.com/views/SpiritualReligiousBeliefsWorldwideMakeoverMonday2025W27/Dashboard1?:language=en-US&:sid=&:redirect=auth&:display_count=n&:origin=viz_share_link
    
    Aesthetic: This visualization is easy to look at because it has a low extraneous
    cognitive load, as it only presents the common radar chart type, does not unnecessarily
    add variation in color, is concise in terms of how the information is presented, 
    and allows the audience to walk through what the visualization is saying easily.
    Plots are shown side by side rather than on different pages, enabling easy 
    comparison. It's 2D, there is a clean layout and there are geometric shapes and lines.

    Substantive: This visualization includes data sources at the bottom and 
    accurately presents percentage of the population which do each of the
    five spirituality characteristics in each country.

    Perceptual: It is easy to tell that the maker of the visualization wants
    to show how different countries, especially the US (from the larger radar plot),
    compare to other countries regarding the five spirituality characteristics
    considered.

    Bad Visualization:
    https://public.tableau.com/views/AdultLGBTQPopulationintheUS/Dashboard1?:language=en-US&:sid=&:redirect=auth&:display_count=n&:origin=viz_share_link

    Aesthetic: The visualization is hard to look at because it adds high extraneous 
    cognitive load because it makes the audience navigate alone through different 
    facts about the LGBTQ population in the US including concentration by 
    state, age, and age distribution by region and bombards you with
    different colors and plot types. 

    Substantive: The information could be presented better in each of the plots.
    For the first one, the values are continuous but the color scale chosen is
    not a graded one - it makes the differences look categorical. For the second
    one, coloring the bar plots in rainbow may be a design choice but it is unnecessary.
    For the third one, all the rainbow arc plot distributions look the same and
    do not reach past the 50% arc.
    
    Perceptual: It is hard to understand what the maker of the visualization is
    trying to convey because of three seemingly separate plots are being presented
    without an explicit linkage. It requires some effort to realize that it is 
    trying to characterize the LGBTQ population the US by showing distribution 
    by region, distribution by age, and distribution by region and age.
    
    ```
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
    ```

    Good Visualization:
    https://public.tableau.com/views/SpiritualReligiousBeliefsWorldwideMakeoverMonday2025W27/Dashboard1?:language=en-US&:sid=&:redirect=auth&:display_count=n&:origin=viz_share_link
    
    Aesthetic: It may be better to move labels out of the radar plots to
    avoid obscuring it.

    Substantive: It may be better to move the axis tick labels closer to the
    axis tick line to be clearer about what is linked with what. Furthermore,
    it might be better to just label the percentage as well on the edges of
    the radar plot to give more quantitative information - I don't think it
    would be too distracting.

    Perceptual: Maybe adding more interpretations to the blurb regarding US
    vs other countries could show more of what the maker of the visualization
    is trying to convey.
    
    Bad Visualization:
    https://public.tableau.com/views/AdultLGBTQPopulationintheUS/Dashboard1?:language=en-US&:sid=&:redirect=auth&:display_count=n&:origin=viz_share_link

    Aesthetic: The visualization could be easier to look at if they did not add
    as much variation in colors and plot types as they did as a design choice - some
    of the variation was not necessary, like the rainbow arc plot versus a simple
    bar plot that was also used. Furthermore, they could explicitly say how the plots
    are linked or add some kind of transition rather than just adding the plots
    with titles and letting the audience figure it out.
    
    Substantive: For the first plot, the color scale chosen could be a single 
    color where the shade is changed to better reflect that it is a continuous
    scale. For the second plot, the bar plots could be a single color. For the 
    third plot, the rainbow arc plots could be turned into bar plots and rescaled
    to emphasize the age differences between regions.
    
    Perceptual: It would be better to link the plots explicitly to make it clear 
    that they are trying to to characterize the LGBTQ population the US by showing 
    distribution by region, distribution by age, and distribution by region and age.
    Better yet, to characterize the LGBTQ population in the US with their data 
    as their message, there might be different plots which are more informative 
    and useful to know.
    ```
