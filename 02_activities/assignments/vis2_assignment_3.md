# Visualization 2

### Dataset: https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/toronto-shelter-system-flow/

- For each visualization, describe and justify: 
    > What software did you use to create your data visualization?

    I used R, specifically the ggplot2 package.

    > Who is your intended audience? 

    Toronto housing policymakers, social service organizations, researchers, and community advocacy groups interested in homelessness trends, specifically regarding newly identified homelessness.
    
    > What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 

    This bar chart shows the number of individuals newly identified as experiencing homelessness each year. It suggests that homelessness is on the decline, and there were specifically low years in 2020 and 2025, indicating that policy changes or other factors may have been effective in reducing it.
    
    > What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 

    I employed aspects of design based on the module materials. The bar chart was designed to convey yearly trends in homelessness while minimizing cognitive load and maximizing accessibility. The chart uses no color to prevent distraction. The simple shape of bars allows viewers to easily compare adjacent years and perceive temporal patterns. Error bars are also provided to more honestly present the data. The chart includes a source annotation at the bottom, signaling transparency and reproducibility. The familiar bar chart format was chosen to communicate exact quantities effectively. The chart supports emotional appeal by presenting yearly counts of newly identified individuals experiencing homelessness. The design  avoids sensationalism, using neutral colors and concise labeling to represent the issue objectively while still eliciting understanding. These choices align the visualization with both presentation and representation functions, it communicates facts clearly while allowing viewers to conceptualize the human impact of homelessness over time.
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 

    The source data is cited and a R script was written - the plot can be reproduced by downloading the data and running the code.
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible? 

    I did not use color as it is unnecessary and adjusted sizing for readability. 
    
    > Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  

    Individuals experiencing homelessness, service providers, local communities affected by housing availability, city policymakers, etc.
    
    > How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 

    I only included the year and total amount of newly identified homeless individuals to make the visualization simple to understand.
    
    > What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?

    This includes data collection by Toronto Shelter & Support Services and Open Data teams, technical work cleaning data, developers supporting R visualization tools, etc.
